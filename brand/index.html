Identity — Draft 1

The wordmark is the logo.

No icon standing in front of the name — "Anqor" set in its own type does the work, the way Runway's logo is just "runway" in a confident geometric sans, nothing else competing for attention. Four options below, same principle, different weight and spacing. Pick one, or tell me what's close but not quite it.

Wordmark options

All four are the same typeface already live on the site (Bricolage Grotesque) — this isn't a font change, it's finding the right weight and spacing for a mark that has to work standalone, not just as a nav label.

Option 1 — Current (baseline)
Anqor

What's live today. Bold, tight tracking, sentence case. Safe, already proven at nav size — the open question is whether it has enough presence to stand alone as a mark.

Option 2 — Lowercase, tighter
anqor

Closest to the Runway reference — all-lowercase, tracked tighter, reads more like a product/tech brand than a studio. More geometric, slightly less "designed."

Option 3 — Heavier, spaced out
ANQOR

Opposite direction — all-caps, heaviest weight available, a touch of breathing room between letters. Reads more authoritative/agency, less startup.

Option 4 — With the arrow mark
Anqor↗

Same as Option 1, but claims the ↗ already used on every CTA across the site ("See it live↗") as part of the mark itself — gives you one small ownable detail instead of a pure text-only logo.

Favicon & icon

Since the wordmark is the whole logo, the favicon can't just be a tiny version of it — "Anqor" is illegible at 16px. It needs a compact mark derived from the wordmark instead.

This is Runway's real icon file, pulled directly from runway.com, not a recreation from memory. It's not a bold letter from their font — it's a custom monoline "R" built from one continuous thick stroke with fully rounded caps and joins, like it's bent from a single piece of rounded tube. That construction technique is what I copied below, not the letter itself.

Built the same way for "A" — one stroked path, not a font glyph — shown at actual favicon sizes (64/32/16px) in both directions you asked for.

Black on cream
White on ink black

Holds up better at 16px than the plain bold-font "A" from the first draft did — the thick uniform stroke stays one solid shape instead of thinning out into fine serifs/terminals at small sizes. Cream-background version for light contexts (browser tabs, light-mode UI), black version for dark contexts — same mark, same file, just the two colorways every favicon set needs.

Wordmark or monogram — which to use where

Rendered at the sizes platforms actually display an avatar. Not a mockup — these are the real files scaled down.

wordmark
monogram

The wordmark holds up at 104px and 56px — a profile page header and a feed/comment avatar, which is where people actually look at it. At 32px it gets tight. The monogram stays crisp all the way down.

Practical call: use the wordmark — it's the stronger brand signal and it's legible at the sizes that matter. Keep the monogram for genuinely tiny contexts (favicon, app icon), which is what it was built for.

Clear space: keep at least the height of the "A" free on all sides of the wordmark. Minimum size: don't set the wordmark below 90px wide — under that, use the monogram instead. Don't recolor, outline, stretch, or add effects to either mark.
