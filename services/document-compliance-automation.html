The problem

The highest-volume work in your business is also the least interesting.

In most operations the largest single consumer of administrative hours is document handling: opening a PDF, reading numbers out of it, checking them against a record, producing a filing, and putting it somewhere. It's repetitive, it's high-volume, and it's where errors are simultaneously most likely and most expensive.

It's also the work people are worst at. Attention degrades across a hundred invoices in a way it doesn't across ten, so error rates climb exactly when volume peaks, which is precisely when you can least afford them.

The technology to read documents reliably has been good for a while. What's changed recently is that models can now handle the messy reality: a scanned photo of a delivery note, a contract with non-standard clause ordering, an invoice in a layout nobody has seen before. Rigid template-based OCR failed on all three.

What hasn't changed is that some decisions shouldn't be automated. We're deliberate about where the human stays in the loop, because a system that files something wrong without anyone seeing it is worse than the manual process it replaced.

How it works

How it works, step by step.

01

Ingestion

Documents arrive by email, upload, shared drive or API. Nothing needs to be renamed or filed by hand first.

02

Extraction

Fields are pulled from the actual document, layout-independent, so a new supplier's invoice format doesn't break the pipeline.

03

Validation against your records

Extracted values checked against what you already hold. Mismatches are flagged rather than silently accepted, which is the single most important design decision in this kind of system.

04

Output generation

Compliant documents produced in the formats your filings, accounting system or portal actually require.

05

Human approval

Anything being submitted externally, anything above a threshold you set, and anything the system wasn't confident about routes to a person first.

06

Routing and archive

Approved outputs delivered into your accounting package, ERP or portal, with the source document and the decision trail retained.

What it isn't

Four things people assume this is.

Template-based OCR

Traditional OCR needs a template per document layout and breaks when a supplier changes their invoice. This reads documents it hasn't seen the format of before.

A system that files things by itself

External submissions sit behind human approval by design. That's what makes the system defensible when someone audits it.

A replacement for your accountant

It removes the data entry. Judgement, treatment decisions and sign-off stay with the person qualified to make them.

A migration project

It reads into the systems you already run. If we're proposing you change accounting software, something has gone wrong with the scoping.

Why we can say this

Why we can say this.

This is a service on our own live site's schema and one of the four systems we've productised. The engineering discipline comes from adjacent work: in our trading systems, financial values use exact numeric types rather than floats and applied migrations are permanent audit records that are never edited. The same care applies here, because in both cases a rounding error or a rewritten history is a real financial event.

Honest limits

What this won't do.

  • It will not make you compliant. It makes compliance work faster and more traceable. Compliance is a determination your policies and your auditor make.
  • Genuinely illegible documents stay illegible. A bad phone photo of a crumpled receipt goes to a human, as it should.
  • It needs your rules written down. If the validation logic lives in one person's judgement, extracting that into explicit rules is part of the work.
  • We won't build fully autonomous external submission. Someone approves before anything is filed.
Questions

Straight answers.

What happens when it can't read a document?

It flags it rather than guessing. Low-confidence extractions route to a person with the original attached. Silent failure is the behaviour we design hardest against in this kind of system, a wrong number confidently entered is worse than no number.

Can this touch regulated filings?

It can prepare them. We keep a human approval step before anything is submitted, deliberately. That's what makes the system defensible in an audit rather than a liability in one.

Where does the document data live?

Wherever your compliance position requires. UAE region, EU region, or entirely inside your own infrastructure with local model inference for sensitive workloads. The data-flow goes in writing before the build starts.

How accurate is the extraction?

High on clean documents, and, more importantly, honest about when it isn't. We tune the confidence threshold with you: route more to humans early, tighten it as you see real performance on your actual document mix.

Can it handle Arabic documents?

Yes, including mixed Arabic-English documents, which is common in UAE commercial paperwork.

How long does implementation take?

Two to three weeks for a first document type running end to end. Additional types are faster once the pipeline exists.
